Data and design

How many populations were sampled? What quality?

Seed for the baseline genomes was collected in 2007–2011 from 55 populations: 347 plants were sequenced, and 330 remain after 17 mislabelled samples were removed. The demographic census followed 20 populations, 18 of them among the 55. Mean sequencing depth in the 15 analysed populations is 6.0–7.7×.

  1. 20 monitored populations the yearly demographic census, 2010–2019
  2. 19 in the demographic analysis Mill Creek is left out: its plots washed out after the 2010 census and were relocated
  3. 15 with baseline genomes Canton Creek and South Fork Middle Fork Tule were not sequenced; Buck Meadows (2 plants) and Carlon (1 plant) fall below the minimum of 4
  4. 12 persisted, 3 extirpated change into the drought and persistence use all 15
  5. 11 with recovery growth change out of the drought: the survivors except NFMF Tule, which has no census interval in the recovery window

How bad was the drought? How was it calculated?

A population’s growth over one census interval is r = log(λ + 0.01), where λ is its projected growth rate for that interval (Sheth & Angert 2018; Anstett et al. 2026). Each outcome averages r over a window of intervals, named here by the year each interval starts.

  • Change into the drought: drought r − pre-drought r
  • Change out of the drought: recovery r − drought r
  • Persistence: 1 = present through recovery, 0 = extirpated during the drought

The offsets’ drought climate is each population’s baseline climate plus its mean 2012–2015 ClimateNA anomaly from the 1981–2010 normal.

The drought index (the bars) combines eight ClimateNA annual anomalies, every variable of the association scan except the extreme maximum temperature: each is standardised across the monitored sites and years and signed so that warmer or drier is positive, the eight are summarised by their median at each site, and each bar is the median across sites. It peaks in 2018 (+0.85), after the recovery window; within 2012–2015 its highest value is +0.59, in 2014.

What climate variables did the genomic offsets use?

Every offset, and the climate benchmark, uses three ClimateNA variables: the water balance of a Mediterranean climate.

CMD
Climatic moisture deficit (Hargreaves, mm): the evaporative demand that precipitation does not meet
Tavesm
Mean summer temperature (June–August, °C): drives evaporative demand
PPTwt
Winter precipitation (December–February, mm): the water supply

The scan’s other six variables are less specific or nearly constant here: annual precipitation (almost all of it falls in winter), summer precipitation (summers are dry), precipitation as snow, and three temperature measures other than the summer mean (annual mean, winter mean and the 30-year extreme maximum).

Climate is standardised once, with the means and standard deviations across the 55 baseline populations: the scale the association scan was fitted on.

Anstett et al. (2026, Table S2) found the same three variables most strongly associated with population decline (OLS p = 0.007 for summer temperature, 0.015 for winter precipitation and 0.051 for CMD; next, annual precipitation, 0.238).

Which loci did the offsets use? What were they compared against?

The candidate loci come from the published genome–environment association scan of the 55 baseline populations (BayPass and window-based tests against nine ClimateNA variables; Anstett et al. 2026).

  1. 605 candidate SNPs, across all nine variables
  2. 287 associated with CMD, summer temperature or winter precipitation
  3. 149 after LD clumping (plink2: 250 kb, r2 0.4, keeping the SNP with the highest Bayes factor)

Metrics 1–3 and 5–8 use these 149 loci; RDAforest (4) uses genome-wide LD-pruned markers, and climate distance uses none.

Random-locus sets

A random-locus version of a metric replaces each candidate locus with a marker from the same minor-allele-frequency bin (width 0.05), drawn without replacement from 16,694 LD-pruned markers (MAF ≥ 0.05, plink2 --indep-pairwise 50 10 0.1). Each drawn marker keeps the climate variable its candidate was assigned.

Each metric is recomputed on 199 random sets (49 for gradient forest, which fits a forest per set). RDAforest has no candidate set, so no random-locus version.

Robustness of the poster’s results

How alike do the offsets rank the populations?

Spearman ρ between every pair of metrics across the 15 populations. Boxes join metrics that hierarchical clustering of the rankings joins at |ρ| ≥ 0.90 (Hmisc::varclus, complete linkage).
